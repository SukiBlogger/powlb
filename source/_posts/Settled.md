As of today, I have been granted "Indefinite Leave to Remain" in the UK. In other words, my status changed from “pre-settled” to “settled” under the EU settlement scheme, as I've lived here for more than 5 years now. In practice, nothing really changes for me at the moment, but it does mean that I can stay longer outside of the UK without compromising my status, and that in a year from now I can apply for UK citizenship if I want to.

I have been lucky enough in my life to never have been subject to any strenuous immigration process (I've never even had to apply for a visa, despite having visited around 15 countries). Having people close to me that have dealt with the worst the system has to offer really makes me appreciate my good fortune in this. All that is to say that this post isn't going to be about me reflecting what I went through to get here — I'll leave that to the people who (sadly) have more interesting stories than mine to offer. 

The way I actually want to mark this day is by celebrating the most British way in which I've changed these past 5 years, which is simply: tea!

So. Much. Tea. 

Tea of so many different kinds and flavours. 

Tea in so many jars, tins, plastic and paper packs, taking over my kitchen counter (and substantial shelf space in my office).

The first time I ever bought tea in my life was a few months into living in the UK. At the time, I was an MSc student renting a private flat with two other students. The flat had a few issues, so for a time we would get frequent visits from the agency's handyman, David. David was quite the character — a man probably in his 70s, Scottish to the bone, friendly, with a sense of humour and a Glaswegian accent which were both incomprehensible to the untrained ear. 

I had many a noteworthy interaction with this quirky handyman over my years there, but for the context of this story it suffices to say that every time David came to our flat, he would ask us for a cup of (black) tea. An utterly normal request of course, as I very well know now!! But back then, I was not yet learnt in the ways of this country and I foolishly had zero tea in my pantry. My flatmates had some tea, but it was fancy loose leaf tea and David was a man of simple pleasures. Of course, coming from a country where hospitality is the most ancient and well-kept tradition, being unable to satisfy my guest's request was heartbreaking —  what would my mother, nay, my grandmother think of me? So the next time I went grocery shopping, the very first thing I bought was a pack of “Scottish Breakfast” teabags (yes, this is a thing in Scotland and it is “specially blended for Scottish water”, which I later learnt is a way of saying “since your water here is better we can give you worse quality tea and it's still fine”[^1]).

So the next time David came round to fix something, I had tea to offer him!! Mission accomplished. Of course, he stopped coming to our flat at some point (not for lack of things that needed fixing, but let's not get started on that), which meant I was left with a whole lot of teabags and nothing to do with them, as drinking them myself didn't seem like a good option at the time. 

Anyway, my love for tea flourished gradually, because of other people and circumstances in my life, but my heart told me I needed to pay tribute to David — and as I often tend to do even when it doesn't seem to make a lot of sense, I followed my heart again.

Now, I can't possibly narrate all tea-related stories from my life in the UK so far, so I might as well get down to business. I'll write down a list of the teas currently in my possession (note that I'll also include herbal tea, which some people don't count as tea but I do). Most of it is loose tea, some of it is teabags, but I won't necessarily specify this. I'll also include brands in some of them, if it feels relevant to me. Here goes:

- **Matcha**, the king of tea. I currently have three tins at home, one from Shibui (Edinburgh-based retailer that you'll see a lot more of here) and two Ippodo ones (Sayaka and Ikuyo) brought to me straight from Japan by two friends. The Ippodo ones are both incredible and probably the best matcha I've ever had — I'm currently running low on them though and trying not to think about it.
- **Cherry black**, the one I had this morning. This one was a birthday gift — it's from another local retailer, but I forget their name name as I've moved the tea from the original packaging to a Bonne Mamman jar a long time ago.
- **Strawberry black**, from Fortnum and Mason, which I got during a London trip.
- **Vanilla rose**, possibly my favourite black one — this one from Shibui.
- **Golden maté**, one of my favourite green blends, again from Shibui. I think I don't have this one at home anymore, but I have some teabags of it in my office.
- **Tropical green**, my other favourite green blend from Shibui, though I only have a teabag or two left from this as well. (I'm actually more of a green tea rather than a black tea person, though I'm not sure this is reflected in my collection).
- **Vanilla chai**, a recent birthday gift, this one from another Edinburgh local, Rosevear. Lately, I'm also experimenting with making my own chai blend from scratch, but admittedly I haven't bought the right (Assam) tea for it yet.
- **Gingerbread sticky chai**, a caffeine-free blend from the same birthday gift, this one from Bird & Blend. It says in the back that it may contain traces of gluten, so I normally wouldn't risk drinking this as a coeliac (oh the horror, to have to check even tea for allergens) but I was assured by Bird & Blend customer support that it's actually safe for coeliacs, so I'll be consuming it until further notice.

[Sidenote: there is absolutely no order in these, other than “this is how they pop into my head” — it will annoy me too when I read this back, knowing I could have ordered them by how much I like them or by how often I drink them or at least grouped them by type of tea or by whether they have caffeine or not — but I'm not going to do this now so bear with me please. I'm also trying not to go to the kitchen while writing this, so that I can see how many I remember without looking at them.]

- **White peony**, a white tea from Shibui.
- **Pai mui tan**, another white tea (which I now learn is the same as white peony, explaining why the two felt so similar..) from the same gift as the cherry black.
- **Jasmine green**, from Shibui, just one last teabag at the office I think.
- **Yellow mountain**, a yellow tea from Shibui (taste-wise I'd say something between white tea and green tea, but I didn't enjoy it as much — I see now that they don't sell it anymore, so I guess other people didn't enjoy it as much either).
- **Mulled wine** — I had two of them, one from Shibui and one from Whittard, but maybe I actually finished those during the Christmas dinner?
- **Caramelised pear**, a Dutch one (brand is Pickwick I think), which I got gifted last year and loved it and now I got it again as a Christmas gift hehe. The person giving it to me originally thought it was a caffeine-free herbal one, but (as my sister discovered!) it's actually a flavoured black tea. The teabags always come with a question, like “What would you do if you had no fear?” or “What makes you happy?”, which of course I refuse to use technology to understand and always make my Dutch friend translate for me.
- **Ginseng**, but just a wee bit left in the jar now. I think my sister gave this one to me.
- **Lavender**, a soothing one, but not a lot left there either.
- **“Clarity” mushroom tea**, a blend of maitake, lion's mane, tulsi, cinnamon, green rooibos and ginger, from tamim teas. I got this one from a museum in Washington, DC.
- **Pumpkin**, a Korean 100% pumpkin tea, a gift from my (Korean) flatmate.
- **Pumpkin spice**, a blend I got from Shibui a long time ago, for making pumpkin spice latte.
- **Chocolate & ginger**, OH MY GOSH I can't believe it took me so long to get to this one. This was possibly “the” tea that got me into tea (yes yes it's not exactly tea but whatever) and definitely “the” tea that got me into Shibui. I had this at a café in Edinburgh and loved it, I saw the brand name on the teabag and proceeded to become a very loyal customer for a long time.
- **Gingerbread**, another Shibui classic for me, which also makes for a great gingerbread latte (I got the recipe for it from the 2022 Shibui advent calendar).
- **Victoria grey**, an Earl Grey blend with lavender from Fortnum and Mason, dedicated to Queen Victoria. I got this one on sale, it's alright but I probably wouldn't buy it again.
- **Marco Polo**, a floral black blend that I quite enjoy. This one is from the French Mariage Frères and it was bequeathed to me by my former (French) flatmate.
- **Yorkshire tea**, some (admittedly quintessential) teabags that were also left here by my former flatmate, which I serve to my guests of simple pleasures.
- **Buket**, a black tea of the Aztea brand, brought to me by a friend from Azerbaijan.
- **Dittany**, as any proud Cretan should. It's great for period cramps and my mom makes sure I never run out of it. In Greek we call it “δίκταμο” (dictamo) but in Crete in particular we more often say “έρωντας” (erontas), which is very similar to the Greek word for (romantic) love, though I can't claim to know why.
- **Chamomile**, which I don't particularly like on its own, but I enjoy it when mixed with dittany (makes for a very soothing blend, as my mom very well knows).
- **Yerba mate**, from Rosevear, though I have to admit I haven't gone into the whole lifestyle of drinking mate properly (yet).
- **White Pitaya**, a green and white blend from Rosevear that I discovered last year and has become one of my staples.
- **Marvellous moringa**, another green blend from Rosevear, though I'm not 100% sure I still have this, as I only had a bit that my sister gave me.
- **Elixir of life**, a herbal blend from Crete that I drink quite regularly in the past few years (just restocked it last week!)
- **Mountain tea**, from Greece — this one was picked up by my aunt if I recall correctly.
- **Linden**, though I personally know it as “φλαμούρι” (flamuri) or “τίλιο” (tilio). In the “soothing herbs I bring from Greece” category.
- **Spice dragon red chai**, a rooibos blend (brand is Stash) I got from some convenience store at Berkeley (there was no tea at the hotel room, will you believe that!!).
- **Elderflower and pear**, a white blend by Good Earth, which I had at a restaurant once and liked (though admittedly at the time I was under the impression it was caffeine-free).
- Various herbal teabags from Greece, including “**Cretan Daylight**” and “**Louisa Darling**” from Thamma tea and “**karteraki traditional**” and “**karteraki chai**” by Dictamus. I also had a **saffron green tea** from Krocus Kozanis, but I think I've ran out.
- **Berry hibiscus**, a fruity blend from Shibui.
- **Lemongrass and ginger**, another herbal one from Shibui.
- **English breakfast**, a lovely yellow tin with the Manchester skyline and iconic bees on it (from New English Teas) — it was a birthday gift from a friend that lived in Manchester at the time. The tea is long gone (though not really consumed by me admittedly), but the tin is now the proud home of all the caffeinated teabags I keep in my office.
- **Oolong cold brew**, which was given to me in a cute small tin with Edinburgh landmarks on it. I don't know the specifics of the tea, as I never saw the original packaging, but it's the only cold brew I have, so it has a special place in my collection (and in my heart, for different reasons).
- **Thailand Oolong**, from Shibui. I'm pretty sure I also had their coconut oolong at some point, but I don't think I have it anymore.
- Various flavours of Numi teabags that I stole from a conference. Obviously I have other teabags too that I'm not going to mention, but I quite enjoyed those ones (and haven't found them in the UK) so I thought they deserved the mention.
- **“Meditation”**, a green blend I brought from Greece (specifically from somewhere in the villages of the Pelion mountain). I'm pretty sure I've run out of this one actually, but really enjoyed it so I thought it was worth the mention. Definitely buying it again if I find it somewhere.
- “**Μαγεία**” (magic), a green blend with pineapple and papaya I brought from Greece.
- **Milk thistle**, one with a rather delicate flavour that I recently brought from Greece. The thistle is Scotland's national flower, yet I don't think I've come across any thistle tea here![^2] I plan to gift this to a group at the university that gathers for tea every Friday, but I haven't given it to them yet so it's still in my possession for now!
- **Tulsi**, a herbal blend introduced to me by a close friend from India. I'm actually surprised at myself I haven't bought any more of this, especially not any loose, as I really enjoy it. For now I only have some happy times tulsi teabags from dragonfly (which I actually don't like that much). But my introduction to tulsi was with the pukka tulsi teabags which I liked way more.
- **Green energy**, teabags from Yogi Tea. I am a yogi after all, so I need some of that energy in my life too. Naturally, I'm also the kind of person who keeps the little tags with the inspirational quotes that come with them. I just moved them from a shelf to my desk yesterday (God knows why) so I have easy access to them now and can quote some of them here:
    - “You are the way you are, that is now.”
    - “Love is subject to compassion.”
    - “Infinity which is your unknown guarantees your happiness” (it look like I have this one twice)
    - “Love without trust is a river without water.”

Okay, the lovey-dovey quotes are probably a good way to end this section. It took a lot longer than I expected (as tends to be the case..) so I'll have to give up and have a quick look at the tea collection in my kitchen before wrapping this up, even though I know I'll later be like “oh I definitely would have remembered this one if I had given me an extra 5 minutes”. But let's spare the excuses for now and see if there's anything important I forgot. 

Ok ok there weren't that many actually, so I'll call it a success. Here are a few that I missed:

- **Apple spice**, a herbal blend I got from my favourite café when it was closing down (originally Jenier tea I think). I've had it for a really long time now and the taste is not that great anymore, even though it used to be nice. I should probably make a final brew and be over with it.
- **“Avondmelange”**, the newest addition to my collection, a herbal blend from the Netherlands that I got as a gift.
- **Evening chai**, a caffeine-free blend from Rosevear that's really loved in Edinburgh.
- Some more caffeine-free teabags, like Whittard's Very Berry Crush or Tipson's moringa & mango.
- Also a couple loose-leaf blends of Cretan herbs, or “καρτεράκι” (karteraki) as we call it there. Oh this also reminds me of “**σαρανταβότανο**” (saradavotano), a traditional 40-herb blend from Crete I used to have.
- **Hazelnut black**, from Rosevear, but only a teeny bit left in an old spice jar.

I've actually been trying really hard lately not to buy new tea until I finish some of the ones I have. I was experimenting and expanding my collection for a long time, but this feels like a good year to settle down on a few favourites (at least a few for my standards!) and enjoy them to the fullest.
