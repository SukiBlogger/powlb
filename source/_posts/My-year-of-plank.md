_A journey of persistence, willpower, and (some say) abs of steel._


On January 1st 2025 I wrote in my newly acquired (thanks sis!) diary using a bright pink pen:

PLANK CHALLENGE BEGINS

I don't remember when exactly I decided to go for it or what was going through my head when I did. But on the first day of the year, the challenge was on. I would do a plank every day, starting with 30 seconds and increasing the duration by 5 seconds each time. 

I do remember _some_ of what I was thinking. I was thinking that back in 2021, during lockdown, I was able to do 5 minutes of plank — was I still able to do that? I didn't know. But instead of just testing it out on the spot, I decided to build my way up to it. Make it more likely to succeed. 

So the first real milestone was to reach 5 minutes. I wasn't really sure what I would do after that, but that goal was clear. And that goal was indeed reached — sometime in March if memory serves me right.* I know for sure it was a Monday. And that's because I decided to turn the whole week into a celebratory, 5-minute-plank week. It wasn't the product of much thought, it just felt like the right thing to do at the time. But the question remained: what happens from now onwards?

One option was to stop. I had accomplished what I had practically set out to do, so it was a good time to stop if I wanted to. But I didn't want to. Now that I had proved to myself I could commit to a task like this every single day, I wanted to carry on. Yet I couldn't really convince myself to keep going at this pace — it didn't feel sustainable in the long run. Yes, I managed to plank for 5 minutes, but I didn't do it _easily_. I was putting a lot of effort into it, both mentally and physically. I would start to tremble a few minutes into the plank and pushing through that final minute was always so hard. My body had already risen up to meet my demands for more than two months straight — disregarding stress, bad moods, period cramps and anything else that stood in its way. But could I really keep asking so much from it? My gut told me I shouldn't.

So I started thinking of ways to maintain the challenge, but adjust the way it scales, to make it more achievable down the line. I could, for example, keep doing 5 minutes of plank every day. I had proved to myself I could do 5 minutes, but could I do it consistently? There is some merit to this variation, but the problem is that it's … boring. Without the incremental change, it registers more like a chore than a challenge to me. I wanted a task that grows every day and builds up to something big. 

So the 5'' increment was staying. But what if I could switch between different types of planks? So far I was always doing a “normal” front plank on my elbows. But if I switched to a high plank or to side planks at regular intervals, surely I could last much longer, right? That thought hit a nerve: it felt both attainable and challenging at the same time. So the challenge was still on and it was here to stay. Next milestone: 10 minutes. 

It was the 1st of May and I was on the phone with a friend when I hit the 10 minute mark. I didn't even struggle that time — my body was so ready for it and the distractions from the conversation made it all the more effortless. And now what? I had considered stopping after the 10 minutes — in a way, it felt like my last opportunity to stop “before it was too late”. But the next day I carried on with my 10 minutes and 5 seconds plank, and that's when I truly knew for the first time that I would do this until the end of the year. 

And so I did. 

At some point, I made the calculations and realised that if I kept up that pace, on the last day of the year I would need to plank for 30 minutes and 25 seconds (or something along those lines). Which felt … rather silly. Or at least anticlimactic. So I decided to make some small adjustments in order to have a beautiful round number to end the year with. By skipping the increment on some days (for example, repeating the 20-minute plank for a few days), I would arrange it so that on December 31st I'd have to plank for exactly 30 minutes. Now that was a nice goal, wasn't it? To plank for half an hour on the last day of the year. 

In the following months, I gradually made the adjustments as planned. The final one was meant for sometime in December, when I was supposed to repeat the 29' plank for a second day. But then I had another idea. What if I skipped this final adjustment? That would mean that I'd reach the 30-minute mark on December 30th. And then on the 31st I could … plank to failure. The moment I thought about it I knew I would go for it, but I hadn't fully accepted giving up my new year's eve to planking just yet. I was in Athens at the time visiting some friends, so I thought I could ask for their opinion on it. Unsurprisingly, my friends voted for planking to failure on the last day of the year. So I planked for 29 minutes and 5 seconds that day and let the ultimate challenge slowly march toward me.

So here I am today. It's December 31st, early afternoon. I'm at my parents' house in Crete, where it all began. There are kids singing carols at our doorstep as I'm writing this. Last night I planked for 30 minutes — yay! And today? Who knows. I have the excuse of my left arm hurting since I got ill last month, but I'd rather not use it. Part of me wishes I can plank for a crazy amount of time — but I'll try to shut down this part while I work on more realistic goals. I think I'd be happy with an hour and ecstatic with anything beyond it. But let's see. No matter what happens today, I am very proud of what I have accomplished this year.

A myriad of thoughts go through my mind as I look back on my year of plank. All the places I planked at, the activities I entertained myself with while planking, the people that accompanied me in this journey — as spectators, supporters, and sometimes even co-plankers. Also the unexpected hurdles that came my way and how I dealt with each of them. Even the ways in which my own body changed. Or the planks themselves, how similar and yet how different they were to each other. The easy planks. The hard planks. And the very, very hard planks. 

But I'll try to save these thoughts for later. For now, I'll go eat something and get ready for my last plank of the year. Wish me luck!
